Animals visible incidentally in a scene would still need an anatomy/component audit before becoming model references.

## Reference and modelling rules

- Inspect one existing source image at a time. Record whether a living form is an actual isolated reference, incidental background or only a written suggestion.
- For flora, distinguish canopy, saplings, shrubs, climbers, flowers, ground cover, aquatic plants and fungi. Fungi remain a separate natural-life category rather than being treated as animal assets.
- Use packed sheets for small insects, birds or plants when silhouettes remain clear. Large animals need fewer subjects and useful front/side/back views; the small-decor packing rule must not force ten large animals into unreadable cells.
- Keep animal anatomy, species identity, body proportions and pose consistent across views. Record wing-open/closed or limb articulation only where needed. A decorative animal can be static; movement is a separate future task.
- Model fauna as characters with topology, rigging, animation and behaviour review when movement is intended. Foliage needs leaf/branch systems, LOD and placement variation. Images alone establish none of these.
- Put small wildlife near human-scale gardens, cottages and ponds. Use quieter populations and local focal moments rather than filling every clearing with animals. Placement density, navigation and runtime performance remain to be tested.
